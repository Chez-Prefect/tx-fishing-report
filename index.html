Public Access Fishing Locations

Ten confirmed public shore/wade access points were mapped across the Indianola → Port O'Connor corridor. All are free, confirmed via multi-source verification including TPWD, community forums, local guides, and GLO records.

  • Indianola Fishing Park — Free public fishing pier with direct access to Matagorda Bay. One of the most popular shore fishing spots in Calhoun County and a favorite for families.
  • Port O'Connor — Boggy Bayou & Little Jetty Area — Shallow flats for walk-in wade fishermen at Boggy Bayou and the Little Jetty area. A public lighted fishing pier is also available at the east end of town.
  • Little Jetties (Walkable Structure) — Confirmed walkable and fishable by community anglers who caught limits of speckled trout using shrimp. The inside east-side wall is best for non-boaters.
  • Port Lavaca Causeway Fishing Pier — One of the longest lighted fishing piers on the Texas coast. Free, open 24 hours, with trout, redfish, drum, and flounder caught regularly.
  • Current Conditions Signal — Recent local reports confirm wade fishing for trout has been strong with oversized fish showing up. Flounder reports include 14 fish in a single wade session with 6 over 16 inches.

Social Chatter & Conditions Intelligence

  • Port O'Connor rated GOOD (TPWD) — Slot redfish inside the north jetty, oversized trout in washouts on shrimp/croaker, bull redfish and jack crevalle schooling between the jetties and Bird Island on Spanish sardines and blue crab.
  • Crowd Flag — POC Big Jetties — Port O'Connor is quiet during the week but heavily crowded on weekends. The big jetties require a 20+ minute boat ride and are completely inaccessible to the boatless angler.
  • Forum Confirmation — A TexAgs user reported arriving at the big jetties to find them crowded, staying only ~30 minutes without catching anything before departing.
  • Indianola Flats Intelligence — Redfish moving into the shallows of Matagorda Bay and the Indianola flats as water temps climb. May–September is prime time for slot reds in the 18–28" range. No boat required.
  • Magnolia Beach / Indianola May Conditions — Speckled trout moving into shallow grass flats with early morning topwater action exceptional in May. Redfish concentrated in back bay waters, cruising grass flats and oyster bars. Flounder beginning to stage near passes and channel edges.
  • Crowd Advantage — Port Lavaca/Magnolia Beach is consistently overlooked by anglers heading to Rockport or Corpus Christi — less pressure on the fish, more room on the water.
  • Flounder Return Confirmed — Captain Pat Lester (Port O'Connor) reports flounder returning to the bays; 14 flounder caught in a single wade trip, six over 16".
